1 · Summary

The system takes a patent number and returns its chemistry as a table. It reads the patent document. It extracts every assay value. It then finds the identity of each compound, because a patent names a compound in several different ways. It checks each structure against the mass the patent printed itself. Today it holds 116,069 assay records from 133 patents, and it has resolved 23,916 compounds to a structure. The largest remaining group is compounds that the patent only draws. That group needs image recognition, and image recognition needs GPU time.

Quote the median, not the pooled figure. Compounds inside one document share a scaffold and a draughtsman, so the sample counts documents rather than compounds.

What the BindingDB figure does and does not say. It compares our value against theirs where both hold a measurement for the same compound. On that overlap we agree on 96.2% at a 1% tolerance, and 99.6% once BindingDB's own rounding to two significant figures is allowed for. That tests the assay reader, and it passes.

It is not a coverage number and it is not a correctness benchmark. Coverage against BindingDB is not yet meaningful: the drawn compounds are not enumerated, so a coverage figure today measures unfinished work. BindingDB and ChEMBL also aggregate from sources outside the patent, so a row we do not hold is not automatically a miss. And identity here is gated by OPSIN, which either parses a name to a structure or refuses — so a compound we report is a compound the nomenclature supports, independent of any reference.

It checks the number, not the name on it. The comparison joins on compound and compares values, so a value can be right and sit under the wrong assay label without the check noticing. BindingDB does record which metric each value is and what the target was, so the label can be tested too. On the 8,790 values where we agree: our metric agrees wherever we state one, and contradicts BindingDB in 3 records — in all three the patent's own column heading says what we say, and BindingDB says otherwise. Our label uses the patent's name for the target and BindingDB uses the protein's formal name (BACE1 against Beta-secretase 1, FLAP against Arachidonate 5-lipoxygenase-activating protein), so the two disagree in wording far more often than in meaning.

The labels worth attention are the ones that name nothing at all — a column headed probe 2, Assay 1 or bare (nM). There are 12 of them, over 10 patents, carrying 1,368 records (1.2%). The value is right; what was measured has to be read out of the patent's prose.

4 · What is extracted, and what is still to run

45,210 compounds carry an assay value. 23,158 of them already hold a structure. Most of the rest are not a failure to read the patent — they are drawings we have not put through recognition yet. That is a compute queue, not a defect. The remaining defects are traced and being fixed. The two are worth keeping apart, because only one of them needs new work.

The 12,892 drawn compounds are the single largest item, and nothing about them is unsolved. A corpus sample of 166 drawings across 33 patents scored a per-patent median of 100% against the masses those patents printed, with 15 of 29 patents at or above 90%. The pipeline reads them correctly.

They are unrun because structure recognition needs a GPU. We currently drive that through Google Colab, which is fine for a sample and does not scale to 12,892 drawings — sessions are interactive, time-limited and billed per session. Recognition is decoupled from the rest of the pipeline, so any compute can do the work: the pipeline writes a job, a machine reads it, the pipeline reads the answers back. What is needed is compute, not code.

Where the remaining work sits

It is concentrated. Of 133 patents, the top 40 hold 91% of everything still outstanding, and the top 10 hold 53%. The pipeline does not read badly across the corpus — it stops on particular documents, which is the cheap kind of problem.

5 · Decisions taken

DecisionReason
No LLM vision for markush structuresThe image recogniser already reads the R-group labels off the drawing. The mapping is a lookup, not a perception problem.
No dedicated markush modelMarkushGrapher-2 scores 48–56% on its authors' own benchmarks. That is below what we already measure.
Model confidence is not a quality signalMeasured on 67 images. Correct and incorrect reads overlap completely.
BindingDB is reference onlyThe only evidence that may correct a structure is what the patent itself printed.
Judge each row, not each tableUnder one plan, 527 of 561 molecules were confirmed and 34 contradicted. The failures were per drawing, so the verdict is per row.
Keep molecules nothing can verifyRefusing them deleted 38 correct molecules on a patent that prints no masses. Absence of evidence is now recorded as such. Evidence against is still fatal.
A grade keeps the patent's range, never a made-up midpointA compound known to be 3–7 nM is a real measurement. Inventing 4.8 nM for it is not, and it would then be compared against references as though it were measured.
Two scales that contest one grade give no rangeSome patents define the same letters twice, once per column. Where the document says which column each governs, each column takes its own. Where it does not, the grade is kept and no range is attached.
Ask what a column measures rather than assumeA column of grades with no heading is almost certainly an assay, and what it measured cannot be read from its cells. Those go to the repair loop as a question. Guessing produced clean records of HPLC methods and peptide sequences.

7 · Next steps

  1. Compute for structure recognition. 12,892 drawn compounds are waiting on GPU time, not on new work. This is the largest single gain available and the method is already measured. Colab does not scale to it.
  2. Unblock the compounds we cannot yet reach. Traced, in progress.
  3. Read the wavy cut bond. 968 compounds are drawn as a scaffold plus a fragment, with a wavy line marking where the fragment attaches. No structure format carries that meaning, so a recogniser fills the gap with a group that is not there. Removing it works; the patent usually explains its own convention in prose, and reading that is the general answer.
  4. Resolve position numbers to atoms. A substituent given as a ring position rather than a drawing, such as 3-CH3. Safe for a simple ring and not yet safe for a fused one, so it is deliberately refused today rather than guessed.
